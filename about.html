Our story

Started with one bag of Colombian caturra in 2019.

Meridian began as a single home roaster with a two-kilo drum in a garage. Seven years later we still roast in small batches — we've just gotten better at finding the farms.

How we work

From cherry to cup, four steps we don't skip

Source

Cup at origin

Our green buyer travels to each farm during harvest and cups the lot before it's purchased — never sight-unseen.

Roast

Log every curve

Each batch is roasted to a written profile, charted from first crack to drop, and archived with the lot.

Cup

Taste before we ship

Every roast is cupped blind against its own profile the same day, before a single bag goes out the door.

Our farms

Long relationships, not spot-market lots

We work with four producers we've bought from for at least three seasons: two smallholder cooperatives in Yirgacheffe and Guji, Ethiopia; a family farm in Huila, Colombia; and a cooperative in Huehuetenango, Guatemala. Prices are negotiated each season and published on the spec sheet for every lot we sell.

  • Guji, Ethiopia Buying since 2020 · washed & natural lots
  • Huila, Colombia Buying since 2021 · natural & honey lots
  • Huehuetenango, Guatemala Buying since 2023 · washed lots
Roasting philosophy

We roast to the bean, not to a house style

A washed Yirgacheffe and a natural Huila don't want the same curve. We adjust charge temperature, development time, and drop point per lot — which is why our roast levels span the full range below, rather than clustering in the middle.
